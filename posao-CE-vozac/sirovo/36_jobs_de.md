## Permit route for a Serbian driver in Germany (realistic picture)

1. Westbalkanregelung (sec. 26(2) BeschV, via sec. 19c AufenthG): Serbia is one of six listed countries; the employer gets BA approval before the visa is applied for at the German mission in Belgrade. Quota up to 50,000 per year; the 2023 and 2024 changes opened it up. The Westbalkan route is the natural fit for a Serbian citizen and does not require a German-recognised qualification.
2. Truck-driver route (sec. 24a BeschV, sec. 19c(1) AufenthG): a third-country driver needs a CE licence (EU/EEA-recognised) plus the EU Grundqualifikation/CPC; BA consent is still needed. Per sources, employers now verify licence and qualification (authorities stopped checking in Nov 2023). A third-country licence is recognised only for a limited period after residence is taken up.
3. Age 45+ (the key issue for this candidate): for first-time approval of an applicant over 45, the applicant must show either gross salary >= 55% of the pension contribution ceiling (2026: 55,770 EUR/year, i.e. 4,647.50 EUR/month) or adequate retirement provision. Sources (Auswaertiges Amt Merkblatte, Bezirksregierung Koeln, TK, Arbeitsagentur page cited for the 2026 figure) say this applies to 24a BeschV driver visas. For the Westbalkan route the sources also list the 45+ rule as applying to first BA approval, and I did NOT find a primary-source exemption for it. Some Westbalkan sources mention exceptions only "in begruendeten Ausnahmefaellen". Treat the threshold as applying unless the BA confirms otherwise.
   - Note the "adequate retirement provision" alternative: this is the only realistic way around the salary bar for a 53-year-old, and it needs documented pension/retirement provision. Ask the BA (Zentrale Auslands- und Fachvermittlung) or a Germany-based migration adviser to confirm before relying on it.
4. Other practical points: no German needed for the visa itself, but the candidate will need German for working life. Driver-licence and CPC steps must be in place; the German mission in Belgrade handles the visa. The 90/180 rule applies to short-stay trips only, not to residence-permit holders working in Germany. Serbian border blockades by Western Balkan drivers in early 2026 (EU EES dispute) are an unrelated but live risk for cross-border work.

Red flags to watch: any employer asking the driver to pay for the visa or BA approval; "free visa" offers; USD-denominated pay; agency accommodation deductions (500 EUR/month seen); pay quoted only as "Spesen" (per-diems are tax-free and not salary, so they do not count toward 4,650).
