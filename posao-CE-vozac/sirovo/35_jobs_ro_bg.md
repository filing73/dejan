## 2. Bulgaria: Bulgarian carriers that hire Serbian / third-country CE drivers and post them abroad

**What I could confirm**

- **Permit route for a Serbian citizen.** A Serbian citizen cannot work in Bulgaria on a free-movement basis. The usual route is the single permit (work permit plus residence), run under the Labour Migration and Labour Mobility Act and the Foreigners Act. The employer applies first to the Employment Agency for approval, which includes a labour-market check (sources say about 15 days). After that the worker applies for a D-type visa at a Bulgarian consulate. Since July 2026 the Employment Agency takes non-EU applications online only, with a qualified electronic signature from the employer (Sofia Globe, 21 Jul 2026). The decision period is unclear: sources say 2–4 months, or about 3 months. A visa takes around 30 working days, often 30–60.
- **Shortage list.** Several commercial sources say truck drivers are on Bulgaria's shortage list, and EURES lists drivers as a shortage occupation (its data is about a year old). That helps the labour-market part, but it does not guarantee approval. I could not find the official 2026 list.
- **Per diem (domestic, not driver-specific).** From 1 Jan 2026 (Decree 354/2025) the domestic per diem is 22 EUR per day with overnight stay and 11 EUR without (previously BGN 40 and 20). The tax-free ceiling is double that: 44 EUR and 22 EUR. Abroad, one source gives 35–80 EUR per day by country for official business trips. Sources do not agree on whether employers can pay up to 200% of the norm or up to 44 EUR, so check the regulation text.
- **Posting rules.** Cabotage and cross-trade are posting cases under Directive 2020/1057, and the host country's minimum wage applies. One law-firm note says per diems and lump sums count toward that. The Commission Q&A says third-country operators may not get better treatment than EU ones. Bulgaria's per-diem tax change moved per diems into taxable wages for some carriers (an industry report, Poland, Romania and Bulgaria), which raises the employer's cost.
- **A1 certificate.** The payroll consultancy source says an A1 is mandatory for all international transport operations, and that inspectors may check CMR notes, tachograph country-code data and payslips. The company must show real activity in Bulgaria (not a post-box company). A1 is a social-security certificate, not a work permit.

**What I could not confirm**

- Any Bulgarian carrier that openly advertises posting Serbian drivers abroad. None of the snippets mention Serbia.
- The typical base-plus-per-diem split. No source gives a breakdown for drivers.
- Rotation (weeks away / weeks home). Not found. The only rule seen in passing is that drivers may not take the weekly rest in the cab (ETF, Mar 2026), and that is an EU rule.

**Pay (as stated, all unverified)**

- Jooble: international C+E at 4,400 EUR/month (no gross/net stated).
- OLX: heavy-goods Sofia at 1,750–1,850 EUR (domestic).
- ITF "Exposed" report (undated in the snippet): drivers from Romania, Bulgaria and Ukraine receiving 310–375 EUR gross/month, sleeping in cabs. This is a red-flag benchmark, not a current ad.

**Red flags to check in any Bulgarian ad**

1. The employer wants a fee, a "visa fee", or a training deposit from the driver.
2. The ad says a free visa or free work permit, with no named process or employer.
3. Salary quoted in USD, or as a total with no split between base and per diem.
4. Per diem paid as a large share of pay, with a low base (that cuts social-security contributions and can fall below the host minimum wage).
5. No A1 and no proof of Bulgarian social-security registration offered.
6. Vague "EU routes" with no named fleet, no CE, or a van-only fleet.
7. Pressure to sign before the Employment Agency approval is in hand.

## 3. Romania: CE driver ads open to Serbian / non-EU drivers

- **Permit route.** Serbia is non-EU. GEO 32/2026 (published 27 Apr 2026) reportedly replaced the old IGI employment permit with an employer-filed visa application (D/AM1 / D/AM2) on WorkinRomania.gov.ro. The transition ran to 7 Aug 2026. The status after that is contested: one source says the platform is live, another (Oct 2026) says employer registration and D/AM2 filing are disabled after a court suspended the shortage list (11 Sep 2026, Order 1073/2026). I could not confirm either point from an official source.
- **Implication.** Truck drivers would most likely need the D/AM2 route through an authorised employer or placement agency. If the shortage list is suspended, that route may be closed for now.
- **Pay.** The eJobs guide gives about 5,500 lei net average for TIR drivers. ANOFM ads are mostly at the minimum wage. Bestjobs CE listings show 3,100–3,500 (currency not stated).
- **Ads found.** No ejobs.ro or bestjobs.eu ad was seen that names Serbian or non-EU candidates, so I have no row I can support as "open to Serbians."

## 4. Assessment (realistic picture)

- Bulgaria is the more likely route for a Serbian CE driver on a Bulgarian contract, since the Bulgarian single permit exists and truck drivers appear on shortage lists. But I found no named carrier that openly advertises this, and the permit timelines are unclear.
- Romania has a process change in 2026 that may be suspended. Confirm the platform status before applying.
- For this candidate (no professional semi-trailer experience, no German), a Bulgarian or Romanian posting is likely to start with a training or mentor placement. None of the sources found show a carrier that offers that explicitly.
- Verify before relying on anything: the Bulgarian Employment Agency and Ministry of Labour (permit and shortage list), WorkinRomania.gov.ro and Monitorul Oficial (Romania), and the Commission's posting Q&A.
