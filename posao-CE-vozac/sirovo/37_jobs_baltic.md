Red flags found:
- Girteka's own page says applications are not processed under migration rules in force since 2024 (it is not clear whether Serbia is affected). Confirm before applying.
- Vlantana's Code 95 and CE training is a driver-paid course (270 EUR or 749 EUR). A charge to a candidate for training is acceptable only if the course is clearly separate from the job offer and the employer does not pay the cost back. Get a written contract first.
- Baltic Transline: TrustBurn (about 20 reviews, 3.1/5) has bait-and-switch complaints. Unverified.
- Smart Enterprises and Transimeksa have age or nationality exclusions.
- Non-EU drivers strike over unpaid wages (trans.info) and Girteka-linked Gretvėja has a Rotterdam wage protest. Not specific to the Baltic ads above.

Serbian citizen permit route (3-5 lines):
- Lithuania: Serbian citizens are visa-free for tourism only (90/180 days). They cannot legally do regular driver work on that basis, and EES rules now treat professional drivers like tourists. Work requires a national D visa plus a temporary residence permit for employment, sponsored by the employer. A 2026 quota of 24,706 was reportedly used up on 7 Sep 2026. Employers can still apply above the salary threshold (about 2,894 EUR gross/month, or about 2,411 EUR for shortage occupations). Secondary sources only; verify with the Migration Department.
- Latvia: no standalone work permit. The right to work is in the residence document issued by OCMA (PMLP). Employer applies first, then Type D visa, then temporary residence permit. Sources give 30-45 working days or 6-8 weeks. A CE licence, Code 95, digital tachograph card and medical are still needed.
- Estonia: temporary residence permit for employment (up to 5 years). Since 1 Jan 2026 the sponsoring employer must be on the Estonian Business Register and show six months of economic activity. The vacancy must be registered with Töötukassa for the labour-market test unless an exemption applies. State fee 250 EUR. The 2026 quota (about 1,292) reportedly fills quickly. Most Estonian CE ads found need Estonian B2, which is a barrier with Dejan's English-only profile.
- Realistic picture: the Baltic employers that take non-EU drivers offer residence-permit sponsorship, and most require 1 year of CE experience, which Dejan lacks. The most realistic entry points are Girteka (Latvia role; check the VFS statement first) and Vlantana's training route. Any route through a paid course should be checked for whether the job is actually guaranteed. Serbian CE jobs (for example Euro Šped in Belgrade, 3,000-3,200 EUR, and OPTIMAL TRANS, 3,300-3,600 EUR, per Infostud) may be a faster alternative to a Baltic route; they are not part of this search and were not verified.
