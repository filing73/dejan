## Permit route for Serbians in Slovenia (realistic picture)

- Serbian citizens are non-EU nationals. They can work in Slovenia only with a work permit and residence permit. Employment is governed by the Slovenia–Serbia bilateral agreement (ESS). The permit is issued to the employer's application, not to the driver (ess.gov.si, delovna dovoljenja). The employer must first post the vacancy on the labour-market register (PDM-KTD form, spot.gov.si) so ZRSZ can check for local candidates. Applications go through the SPOT portal or the administrative unit. The state fee quoted is 70 EUR (spot.gov.si). The driver should not pay this.
- Timing: one recruiter source estimates 2–5 months from complete application to permit, and says the driver must not travel to start work before the approval or card is issued. Treat this as a rough estimate; ZRSZ gives 15 days for its own decision, but other sources say 60 days.
- Licence: C+E and Code 95 are required. An EU-recognised KOD95 is valid. A non-EU or foreign-issued code usually requires a Slovenian course and knowledge test with an authorised provider. Your expired Slovenian Code 95 and the Serbian CPC (Nov 2026) may both help, but this must be confirmed with ZRSZ or AMZS before you pay for anything. Also apply the tachograph driver card and medical certificate.
- Realistic picture: C+E with no semi-trailer experience and no German means the best matches are employers that name a permit route and accept Serbian passports (Silos, Partners & Orka). Expect roughly 3–5 months to a first trip, and budget for a KOD95 course if the Serbian CPC is not recognised. The Silos "3 years" requirement is the main barrier. A mentor or training stint is the realistic route in.
