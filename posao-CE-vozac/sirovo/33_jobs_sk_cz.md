## Permit route and realistic picture

- **Slovakia:** Truck driver for international transport is on the shortage list. The ASR barometer counts 18,286 open positions for this role in 2026. The main route is the national "D" visa for drivers from listed third countries. Trade and press sources give a 2026 cap of up to 5,000 visas, valid up to one year and tied to one employer. Serbia is reported as eligible (trade press, not an official list). Non-EU drivers also need the Regulation 1072/2009 certificate, valid up to 5 years. Employers may not have more than 45% third-country staff. These figures come from secondary sources, so check the Ministry of Labour (employment.gov.sk) before relying on them.
- **Czech Republic:** No truck-driver-specific permit was found. The route is the employee card (zaměstnanecká karta), tied to one employer and position, usually 2 years, with a decision in 60 days (up to 90). A 2025 Chamber of Commerce document lists Serbia among eligible countries, but that list must be checked against mvcr.cz for 2026. The "Kvalifikovaný zaměstnanec" programme named in the brief was not confirmed by any search result, so treat it as unverified. Since 1 Apr 2026 employers report through JMHZ.
- **Prerequisites that will matter most:** Most ads ask for Code 95 (CPC) and a driver tachograph card. The user's Serbian CPC card is due Nov 2026, so the Serbian card's recognition in each country needs confirming. The lack of semi-trailer experience and no German will narrow the field. Ask employers directly whether they train beginners and whether they will handle the permit.
- **Realistic picture:** Few ads state openness to non-EU drivers. The best-supported leads are the Slovak direct employer (D.L.B.) and the Czech ads that say foreigners are accepted. Expect long permit timelines (weeks to months), employer-bound visas, and a need to confirm each employer's policy by phone or email before any travel. Search results cannot confirm current pay, dates or the employers' current hiring.
