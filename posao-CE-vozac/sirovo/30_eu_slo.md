## Required answers
**(a) Work-permit route for Serbians (official ZRSZ pages):** Serbia has a bilateral agreement. The employer (not the worker) applies to ZRSZ for a work permit; it counts as consent to the single residence-and-work permit issued by the upravna enota (administrative unit); the worker collects the permit via the Slovenian embassy/consulate in Serbia (employer can do this with authorisation). Conditions: age 18+, registered as unemployed with NSZ Serbia, no suitable domestic candidate for the vacancy, employment contract at least 1 year. The agreement permit is valid 3 years, renewable 3 more. Employer must register him for social insurance within 15 days of receiving the permit. Renewal form TUJ-SRB-2. Non-visa entry is allowed for Serbians. The generic single permit (Foreigners Act changes in force since 21 May 2025) is up to 2 years first time, 3 years on renewal. An aggregator reported a "new rules" article (Data d.o.o., June 2026) but I could not see any actual change; verify at ess.gov.si before relying on it. Sources: https://www.ess.gov.si/en/jobseekers/employment-of-non-eu-migrant-workers/work-in-slovenia/employment-of-nationals-of-serbia ; https://www.ess.gov.si/iskalci-zaposlitve/zaposlitev-tujih-drzavljanov/delo-v-sloveniji/zaposlovanje-drzavljanov-srbije/ ; https://data.si/blog/zaposlovanje-drzavljanov-srbije-po-novem/

**(b) Renewing expired Slovenian Code 95:** Per Slovenian providers, Code 95 is valid 5 years; renewal = 35 h periodic training (5 x 7 h). If already expired, you must complete the 35 h for the five years back from the year you renew (Slovenian text: "po programih določenih za pet let nazaj"), so it is possible, not a full re-exam. Price: about 50 EUR per 7 h block (koda95.si, Formula), so about 250 EUR total; a 2020 CPU listing at 40 EUR is outdated. Providers: AMZS, OZS, GZS, B&B, Prah, Blisk, koda95.si. NOT found: any Slovenian source stating whether a Serbian resident can take the course or how the code is entered on a Serbian licence. Ask a provider and the upravna enota first; most providers expect a licence/residence link to Slovenia, and a Slovenian employer normally arranges this. Sources: https://bb.si/podaljsanje-kode-95-redna-usposabljanja/ ; https://koda95.si/storitve_in_cenik/ ; https://www.formula.si/poklicni-vozniki/

## Notes
- Realistic picture: the Serbia agreement route is workable and fairly quick (Balkandrive claims 1-3 months, an intermediary claim), but it needs a direct employer willing to file with ZRSZ and a candidate registered with NSZ Serbia. Foreign drivers are common (Zurnal24: 60% of 27,082 foreign drivers employed drive heavy trucks), so demand exists.
- Almost every Slovenian ad demands CE + valid Code 95 + often 1 year experience; true beginner ads (Jurčič) are rare. Beginner plus permit help together was not found with any direct employer.
- Pay/rotation: not verified from a source; do not assume. Red flags: ads in USD with "free visa", agencies charging fees, fake aggregator listings.
- Practical step: email Jurčič and Kobal asking both questions in writing; renew Code 95 only after a job offer states it will be recognised.
